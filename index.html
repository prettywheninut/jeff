Every Indian girl has a multani mitti story. Mine lives in a steel dabba on my mother's dressing table, next to a comb with three teeth missing and a bottle of rose water that never quite emptied. Sunday morning, before the pressure cooker started its whistle, she would mix the pale brown powder with a little curd, smear it on my face, and tell me to sit still. "Ten minutes. Don't smile."

The mask would crack. My skin would feel tight enough to audition for a drum. And yet — for about two hours afterwards — my face looked genuinely, unmistakably brighter. That was the magic. That was also the problem.

The dabba was never the problem. The formula was.

Multani mitti — Fuller's earth — is brilliant at exactly one thing: absorbing. It drinks up sebum, sweat, sunscreen residue and the day's pollution. For oily, acne-prone Indian skin, that feels like a reset button.

But clay doesn't know the difference between the oil you want gone and the moisture your skin barrier needs. Dermatologists call it a "double-edged sword": use it too often, leave it on too long, and it strips the very lipids that keep your skin calm. The result? Flaking. Sensitivity. A weird cycle where your skin overproduces oil because it's dehydrated.

"The same absorbent property that makes multani mitti great for oily skin can be detrimental for dry or sensitive skin."

— Dr Jatin Mittal, cosmetologist, in The Indian Express

So we kept the clay. We just stopped letting it work alone.

01

It over-absorbs

Plain clay pulls oil and water. Ten minutes past the point of comfort, your barrier is compromised.

02

It's a guessing game

How much powder? How much liquid? Every kitchen batch is a different strength. Your skin deserves consistency.

03

It's one-size-fits-all

Oily T-zone, dry cheeks, hormonal chin — Indian skin is rarely just one thing. A single clay can't do all three.

Is multani mitti safe for dry or sensitive skin?

Plain clay can be too drying — that's the whole reason we buffered it. Rose Glow is formulated for combination and dry-leaning skin, and we recommend mixing it with curd or rose water instead of plain water. If you have eczema or rosacea, patch-test first and check with your dermatologist.

How often should I use it?

Once a week for most skin types. Oily, acne-prone skin can go twice a week if your barrier is happy. Never leave it on longer than 10 minutes, and always remove it while it's still slightly damp.

Will it lighten my skin tone?

No. And we'd rather be clear about that than sell you a lie. Revive Plus contains no bleaching agents. It lifts surface oil, dullness and mild tan so your natural tone looks clearer and more even. It will not change the colour you were born with — and it shouldn't.

Is it tested on Indian skin?

Yes. Every batch is dermatologist-tested and clinically assessed on Indian skin types across four humidity zones. It's non-comedogenic, paraben-free, sulphate-free and never tested on animals.

Can I use it during pregnancy?

The formula is free of retinoids, salicylic acid and essential-oil heavy fragrance, so it's generally considered pregnancy-safe. That said, every pregnancy is different — please run the ingredient list past your doctor first.

What's the full ingredient list?

Rose Glow: Fuller's Earth (Multani Mitti), Kaolin, Rosa Damascena Flower Water, Aloe Barbadensis Leaf Juice, Glycerin, Santalum Album Powder, Crocus Sativus Extract, Panthenol, Sodium Benzoate, Potassium Sorbate. That's it. No fragrance, no dyes, no fillers.
